# Catan agent integration fit for Empires

**Date:** 2026-09-05

**Status:** current-source integration audit; no algorithm or production policy selected
**Scope:** Empires' rules, policy contracts, simulator, trading, and iOS runtime versus the public candidates named in the [AI strategy research program](2026-09-05-ai-strategy-research-program.md). This report estimates engineering boundaries; it does not normalize incomparable win rates.

## Executive finding

No reviewed public agent is a drop-in Empires policy.

The closest reusable artifacts are Eli6th's MIT-licensed 4-player MLP checkpoint and nogulong's MIT-licensed 4-player ONNX models. Both still require a semantic state/action adapter, and neither was trained on Empires' rules. Eli6th's strongest `AlphaBot` is not merely a checkpoint: its search clones and advances its own Rust engine, so reproducing that agent inside Empires crosses from a policy port into a second-engine/search port. Nogulong's reactive model is a narrower possible policy port, but its action, observation, discard, robber, Road Building, and targeted-trade contracts differ.

Catanatron and JSettlers2 are useful external engines and comparator lineages, not embeddable policies. The published Gendre–Kaneko, Szita–Chaslot–Spronck, and STAC dialogue results are evidence for architectures or component boundaries; they do not supply a complete compatible checkpoint. Charlesworth supplies a real full-game checkpoint, but its missing repository license prevents product reuse without permission and its old Python/PyTorch stack is not an iOS runtime.

Monte Catano is an early Rust engine with source MCTS, not a learned or complete reusable policy. It omits player trading and a finite resource bank, auto-selects discards, has material award/road/robber mismatches, and its arbitrary-position UCI path ends in `todo!`. Its demo deliberately spends five seconds on each non-forced decision; lower budgets are configurable but unvalidated, and no iPhone result exists. AGPL-3.0 is a hard product-boundary warning: keep it out of the app and out of an undisclosed modified service unless counsel deliberately approves the resulting obligations. An isolated, unmodified research executable is the only boundary worth considering, and even that would benchmark a different game.

The smallest honest next comparison is therefore not “drop in AlphaBot.” It is a two-candidate adapter bake-off: reproduce Eli6th and nogulong unchanged first, then compare their **reactive** checkpoints through narrow Empires-native adapters, with the same frozen fallback for unsupported decisions. Empires remains the only transition and legality engine. Anything stronger would first require evidence that the adapters preserve semantics.

Three boundaries are kept distinct:

- **Policy port:** Empires creates the observation and legal mask, foreign inference ranks choices, and Empires applies the selected `GameMove`. No foreign state transition is authoritative.
- **Engine replacement/search port:** the candidate must clone or advance its own state to choose a move. This duplicates rules and requires differential state-transition validation.
- **External benchmark:** the candidate remains a separate executable playing its own rules, or receives only scenarios representable in both engines. Its win rate is not directly comparable to Empires self-play.

“Checkpoint usable” below means that a public artifact can be loaded by its own current source. It does **not** mean its neurons have Empires meanings.

## Empires' actual integration contract

### Rules and state transitions

Empires is a deterministic 3- or 4-player base-game engine with configurable 8, 10, or 12 VP targets. New games create the normal 19-resource-card banks and 25-card development deck ([`GameState.swift`](../../Packages/CatanEngine/Sources/CatanEngine/Models/GameState.swift#L219-L285)). Setup is a forward/backward snake draft, and the second settlement grants adjacent resources ([`SetupPhase.swift`](../../Packages/CatanEngine/Sources/CatanEngine/SetupPhase.swift#L1-L55), [`SetupPhase.swift`](../../Packages/CatanEngine/Sources/CatanEngine/SetupPhase.swift#L101-L132)).

The legal authority is `RulesEngine.legalMoves`, covering setup, pre-roll development cards, rolling, main-turn builds/cards/trades, out-of-turn trade responses, seat-scoped discards, and robber tile/victim combinations ([`RulesEngine.swift`](../../Packages/CatanEngine/Sources/CatanEngine/RulesEngine.swift#L7-L104), [`RulesEngine.swift`](../../Packages/CatanEngine/Sources/CatanEngine/RulesEngine.swift#L179-L207)). Production implements the official all-or-nothing shortage rule for multiple claimants, building enforces supply/distance/connectivity, Road Building is an ordered atomic pair, and robber victims must both border the tile and hold a resource ([`MainPhase.swift`](../../Packages/CatanEngine/Sources/CatanEngine/MainPhase.swift#L1-L73), [`Building.swift`](../../Packages/CatanEngine/Sources/CatanEngine/Building.swift#L8-L96), [`DevCards.swift`](../../Packages/CatanEngine/Sources/CatanEngine/DevCards.swift#L47-L104), [`Robber.swift`](../../Packages/CatanEngine/Sources/CatanEngine/Robber.swift#L15-L69)). Longest Road accounts for blocking and holder/tie semantics; victory is checked after actions against the configured target ([`LongestRoad.swift`](../../Packages/CatanEngine/Sources/CatanEngine/LongestRoad.swift#L1-L79), [`WinCondition.swift`](../../Packages/CatanEngine/Sources/CatanEngine/WinCondition.swift#L1-L42)).

This matters because a search agent cannot be transplanted by translating only its root position. Every simulated successor, chance outcome, award transfer, shortage, and subphase must agree or the search evaluates a different game.

### Live observation and action seam

The narrow production seam is `Policy.selectMove(observation:)`, but `GameObservation` currently contains the complete `GameState` plus exact legal moves ([`GameSession.swift`](../../Packages/CatanEngine/Sources/CatanEngine/GameSession.swift#L3-L53)). Therefore a live policy can peek unless its adapter enforces a restricted view. The versioned encoder can emit either `revealAll` or shape-compatible `publicCountsOnly`, but that restriction currently governs encoding/export rather than the raw live object ([`StateEncoding.swift`](../../Packages/CatanEngine/Sources/CatanEngine/StateEncoding.swift#L219-L297)).

The fixed vector is layout v3 and 5,182 floats. Its board geometry is the usual 19 tiles, 54 vertices, and 72 edges; 3,840 slots are reserved for up to 256 pending offers ([`StateEncoding.swift`](../../Packages/CatanEngine/Sources/CatanEngine/StateEncoding.swift#L47-L97), [`StateEncoding.swift`](../../Packages/CatanEngine/Sources/CatanEngine/StateEncoding.swift#L145-L217)). It deliberately excludes RNG position, development-deck order, schema metadata, and `robberMover`, so it is a policy observation rather than a lossless engine-state interchange ([`StateEncoding.swift`](../../Packages/CatanEngine/Sources/CatanEngine/StateEncoding.swift#L17-L46)).

The typed action is `GameMove`, not an integer ([`Move.swift`](../../Packages/CatanEngine/Sources/CatanEngine/Models/Move.swift#L10-L27)). Layout v2 maps it to a fixed 9,335-way four-chair head with large ordered Road Building and discard segments ([`ActionSpace.swift`](../../Packages/CatanEngine/Sources/CatanEngine/ActionSpace.swift#L61-L155)). That codec is not a complete serialization of every engine-legal move:

- its bank-trade slot represents one give resource and one receive resource even though the engine accepts balanced mixed bundles ([`ActionSpace.swift`](../../Packages/CatanEngine/Sources/CatanEngine/ActionSpace.swift#L328-L343), [`Trading.swift`](../../Packages/CatanEngine/Sources/CatanEngine/Trading.swift#L26-L105));
- its proposal slots represent one resource kind per side with bounded quantities ([`ActionSpace.swift`](../../Packages/CatanEngine/Sources/CatanEngine/ActionSpace.swift#L345-L367));
- discard bundles stop at ten cards ([`ActionSpace.swift`](../../Packages/CatanEngine/Sources/CatanEngine/ActionSpace.swift#L378-L395)); and
- decoding a proposal fabricates proposer seat 0, so an adapter must restore the acting seat before application ([`ActionSpace.swift`](../../Packages/CatanEngine/Sources/CatanEngine/ActionSpace.swift#L209-L251)).

The mask intentionally traps if a legal move has no encoding ([`ActionSpace.swift`](../../Packages/CatanEngine/Sources/CatanEngine/ActionSpace.swift#L254-L281)). A foreign policy adapter should therefore translate the typed `legalMoves` presented at runtime, not assume all engine behavior is captured by 9,335 logits.

### Simulator and checkpoint contract

`GameSession` is shared by the app and headless harness. It chooses the next actor, creates seat-specific legal moves, calls the policy, rejects a non-legal return, and applies through `RulesEngine`; a 25-policy-action turn backstop forces `endTurn` after evaluating the policy ([`GameSession.swift`](../../Packages/CatanEngine/Sources/CatanEngine/GameSession.swift#L55-L105), [`GameSession.swift`](../../Packages/CatanEngine/Sources/CatanEngine/GameSession.swift#L281-L325)). Session checkpoints serialize state, control flow, RNG, and telemetry, but explicitly do not serialize executable policy objects ([`GameSession.swift`](../../Packages/CatanEngine/Sources/CatanEngine/GameSession.swift#L114-L200)).

The CLI simulator supports seeded 3/4-player matrices, 8/10/12 targets, standard/random boards, and a 3,000-action cap, but it constructs only built-in heuristic or random policies; there is no model-loader or foreign-policy protocol ([`sim/main.swift`](../../Packages/CatanAI/Sources/sim/main.swift#L5-L65), [`sim/main.swift`](../../Packages/CatanAI/Sources/sim/main.swift#L118-L197), [`sim/main.swift`](../../Packages/CatanAI/Sources/sim/main.swift#L363-L430)). Export examples carry exact masks, the 5,182 features, observer, rules/layout provenance, and terminal value ([`TrainingExample.swift`](../../Packages/CatanAI/Sources/CatanAI/TrainingExample.swift#L12-L39), [`TrainingExample.swift`](../../Packages/CatanAI/Sources/CatanAI/TrainingExample.swift#L41-L118)). This is enough to build an adapter, not enough to load any reviewed checkpoint unchanged.

### Empires trade semantics

Empires has two distinct trade behaviors that an adapter must not conflate.

At engine level, an offer records proposer, give, and want but no target seat ([`Move.swift`](../../Packages/CatanEngine/Sources/CatanEngine/Models/Move.swift#L30-L41)). Any non-proposer may accept or reject; acceptance swaps resources, while a single rejection removes the offer globally ([`Trading.swift`](../../Packages/CatanEngine/Sources/CatanEngine/Trading.swift#L107-L179)). The generic session asks responders in seat order, queues the first acceptance, and otherwise queues one rejection ([`GameSession.swift`](../../Packages/CatanEngine/Sources/CatanEngine/GameSession.swift#L391-L459)). The human-facing app instead polls every bot's heuristic and lets the human select among acceptors ([`GameViewModel.swift`](../../Settlers/ViewModels/GameViewModel.swift#L812-L885)). Neither path models official counteroffers or a proposer confirmation among multiple acceptors.

Proposal validation requires positive quantities and proposer affordability but does not require non-empty maps or disjoint resource kinds; empty or same-resource bilateral offers can therefore pass the engine even though current UI/policy generators avoid them ([`Trading.swift`](../../Packages/CatanEngine/Sources/CatanEngine/Trading.swift#L123-L138)). Enumerated policy offers are narrower still: one give type in quantities 1–3 for one want type in quantities 1–2, capped at 120 candidates, with three declines per turn ([`RulesEngine.swift`](../../Packages/CatanEngine/Sources/CatanEngine/RulesEngine.swift#L106-L176)).

Consequently, foreign trade behavior must be classified as one of: exact translation, a deliberately reduced common subset, or unsupported fallback. Silently mapping a targeted offer, counteroffer, broadcast response round, or bundle trade to Empires' one-offer/one-rejection protocol changes the policy.

### Device/runtime boundary

The shipping target is native Swift 6 on iPhone/iOS 17, with `CatanEngine` and `CatanAI` as Foundation-only local packages and no Python, Rust, Java, ONNX Runtime, Core ML model, or server dependency ([`project.yml`](../../project.yml#L1-L56), [`CatanEngine/Package.swift`](../../Packages/CatanEngine/Package.swift), [`CatanAI/Package.swift`](../../Packages/CatanAI/Package.swift)). The app constructs in-process `HeuristicPolicy` instances ([`GameViewModel.swift`](../../Settlers/ViewModels/GameViewModel.swift#L1037-L1052)). Its 2–4 second bot delay is presentation pacing, not evidence of available inference budget ([`GameViewModel.swift`](../../Settlers/ViewModels/GameViewModel.swift#L1151-L1167)).

A deployable port therefore needs Swift/Core ML inference or a deliberately approved native library, deterministic fallback, model packaging, memory/thermal measurement, and target-iPhone p50/p95/p99 latency. Desktop throughput and simulator steps per second do not answer that question.

## Adaptation consequences by layer

### Engine replacement is the wrong default

Eli6th AlphaBot, Catanatron search, Szita-style MCTS, Charlesworth forward search, and Monte Catano all need successor states. If they advance their own engines, validation must compare at least setup grants, every legal-mask transition, finite-bank shortages, discard ownership, robber victim eligibility/random steal, development-card maturity and deck chance, Road Building continuation, road blocking/ties, Largest Army, trade lifecycle, and victory timing. A root-state converter without this differential suite produces confident decisions about a different game.

An Empires-native search implementation behind `Policy` avoids that duplicate-authority problem. That is an adaptation category, not a recommendation for a particular search algorithm.

### A checkpoint port is semantic, not dimensional

None of the reviewed checkpoints has 5,182 inputs and 9,335 outputs. Padding, truncating, or adding a final linear layer cannot preserve meanings. A legitimate port needs:

1. a documented coordinate bijection for all 19/54/72 board locations;
2. actor-relative seat and resource-order translation;
3. exact visibility handling that never exposes opponent secrets in production;
4. phase/subphase translation, including compound versus sequential actions;
5. per-legal-`GameMove` foreign logit mapping, with explicit unsupported cases;
6. checkpoint version/hash and numerical parity against the author's runtime; and
7. retraining whenever the foreign policy's rule assumptions materially determine value.

Eli6th and nogulong are the only current artifacts that justify paying this validation cost. Charlesworth would join that set only if reuse permission is obtained.

### License is part of the adaptation boundary

MIT code/artifacts still need attribution and provenance review, but Eli6th and nogulong do not impose the reciprocal source boundary of the GPL family. Catanatron, PeterLP, JSettlers2, and STAC should stay as separate research executables unless Empires deliberately accepts GPL obligations. Monte Catano is more restrictive for the contemplated service escape hatch: AGPL also addresses users interacting with modified covered software over a network. This report is an engineering risk boundary, not legal advice; embedding, linking, translating source, shipping a derivative, or hosting a modified agent requires counsel before work begins. Keeping an unmodified executable separate reduces coupling but does not erase its license.

## Ranked integration-feasibility shortlist

This ranks the **smallest decision-relevant integration step**, not expected playing strength. Criteria are public runnable artifact, 3/4-player proximity, bounded state/action translation, usable license, and deployable-runtime path. Published win rates were not used as interchangeable scores.

1. **Eli6th reactive PPO — external reproduction, then conditional policy port.** It has the best artifact contract: current source, two loadable checkpoint forms, fixed/versioned I/O, compact framework-free inference, and MIT terms. First-to-7, perfect visibility, and trade protocol prevent a drop-in claim. `AlphaBot` is explicitly outside this first port because it crosses the engine boundary.
2. **nogulong reactive ONNX agents — external JSettlers reproduction, then conditional policy port.** It ships small standard-format models and uses public-information observations. It ranks below Eli6th because the cross-dimensional input, two-model trade path, fixed detailed action heads, rule mismatches, and Python/ONNX runtime create more translation and deployment work, while published strength/provenance remain incomplete.
3. **JSettlers2/STAC — external comparator only.** It is mature and closest to the published comparator/trading literature, but its Java server/message brain and GPL license make product integration infeasible. Use it to challenge claims, not to become Empires' policy.
4. **Catanatron — external rules/scenario baseline only.** It is easier to script than JSettlers and useful for differential scenarios, but no current learned checkpoint and GPL licensing leave nothing appropriate to ship.
5. **Charlesworth — external checkpoint run, conditional on environment reconstruction; product use blocked pending permission.** It is the next most relevant complete policy, but an absent license and ten-second default search path outweigh its four-player/10-VP similarity.

Not shortlisted: the paper-only agents require reimplementation; PeterLP and BenjaminL1 intentionally solve 1v1 variants; SamiKoneru has no public checkpoint/license and its trade path is outside its policy action contract; Monte Catano is an AGPL early engine with material rule gaps and a broken arbitrary-state interface.

## Smallest honest bake-off

The first bake-off should answer **adapter feasibility**, not crown a strongest algorithm.

### Arms

- `H`: frozen shipping `HeuristicPolicy`.
- `H + E`: the same policy, replacing only exactly mapped decisions with Eli6th's reactive PPO logits.
- `H + N`: the same policy, replacing only exactly mapped decisions with nogulong's main/trade ONNX logits.

All unsupported decisions use the same frozen `H` fallback and are counted by reason. Search is off in every arm. Empires alone enumerates legality and applies transitions. This makes the measured difference attributable to a policy adapter rather than a foreign engine.

### Gate 0 — unchanged reproduction

Pin source commits, model SHA-256 values, dependency locks, commands, hardware, rules, target score, visibility, seeds, and raw per-game results. Reproduce each candidate in its own engine. Failure to load, complete, or approach its documented result ends that adapter; it is not repaired by changing Empires.

### Gate 1 — translation conformance

Use a small frozen corpus of Empires states covering both setup directions, pre-roll, ordinary main play, each development card, finite-bank edge cases, discard, robber with zero/one/multiple eligible victims, Longest Road blocking, and trade propose/respond. For every state and chair:

- round-trip board coordinates, seats, resource order, phase, visible/private fields, and every mapped action;
- prove every emitted move is an element of that state's typed Empires `legalMoves`;
- compare foreign-runtime logits with the deployment-runtime logits to a declared tolerance;
- record unsupported legal moves, fallback decisions, and states that cannot be represented—never coerce them; and
- run public-information and reveal-all adapters as separate experiments. Only public-information results are product-eligible.

No gameplay strength claim is made at this gate.

### Gate 2 — paired feasibility games

Run standard-board 10-VP games at both supported table sizes, every chair, on untouched common seeds. Use 32 seed groups per table size: 96 three-player and 128 four-player games per arm, 224 per arm total. This is deliberately a gross-regression screen, not a powered promotion study. Report each table size separately with clustered paired confidence intervals and the correct 1/3 or 1/4 fair-share reference.

Required outputs are completion/cap rate, illegal-action count, fallback/unsupported rate by phase, win and VP distributions, move count, trade offer/acceptance rate, and Mac plus target-iPhone p50/p95/p99 decision latency, model size, peak memory, and thermal notes. Preserve exact trajectories for any disagreement or failure. Do not pool 3- and 4-player percentages.

### Decision after the bake-off

- A candidate that cannot reproduce, maintain zero illegal moves, or execute numerically equivalent inference is not portable.
- A high fallback rate means the result describes a heuristic hybrid, not the public agent; it cannot support a claim that the checkpoint transferred.
- Feasibility-game win rates are descriptive. Before saying an arm is stronger, precompute the required sample from the observed paired variance, freeze a new held-out seed set, retain every-chair rotation and both table sizes, and run the repository's bot-strength protocol.
- Only after a reactive adapter survives should an Empires-native bounded-search arm be added. Foreign-engine search and Monte Catano are not part of this smallest bake-off.

That design is the smallest comparison that preserves Empires' rules authority, exposes rather than hides unsupported semantics, and can falsify checkpoint portability before funding engine duplication or retraining.
