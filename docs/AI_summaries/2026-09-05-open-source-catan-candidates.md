# Open-source Catan-playing agents: evidence audit as of 2026-09-05

> **Selection update, 2026-09-05.** After this cross-project audit, newer
> public branches of `Eli6th/catan-rl` were discovered and run. Commit
> `7046c6b` publishes a V5A-derived first-to-10 realistic-information model
> that is a runnable later candidate, not a proven better baseline: initial
> comparisons changed engines and seed schedules. The original `main` model
> remains the reproduction priority. Exact
> hashes, balanced results, and evidence limits are recorded in
> [`2026-09-05-catan-rl-upstream-artifact-audit.md`](2026-09-05-catan-rl-upstream-artifact-audit.md).

## Executive conclusion

There is no defensible, variant-independent "strongest open-source Catan agent" in the public evidence audited here. The reported percentages measure different games: two versus four players, 7 versus 10 versus 15 victory points, perfect versus hidden information, ordinary versus modified dice, and absent, bounded, or full player-to-player trading. They also use different opponents and evaluation budgets. This memo therefore does **not** turn those percentages into a leaderboard.

The evidence-weighted conclusions are narrower:

- **Eli6th/catan-rl is the leading public 2026 four-player learned-agent artifact candidate**, because it publishes executable weights and reports an 82% win rate against three fixed heuristic opponents. The result is first-to-7 with perfect information, however, not ordinary first-to-10 hidden-information Catan; AlphaBot's logged sample size conflicts across the repository; the documented command silently uses the engine's 10-VP default; the raw logs are absent; and the checkpoint names an engine commit that is not in the public history.
- **PeterLP123/catanatron-1v1 has the best evaluation discipline in this set**, including frozen parents, shared schedules, seat splits, confidence intervals, rejection of failed ideas, and machine-readable summaries. It studies a bespoke two-player, 15-VP, balanced-dice ruleset; the retained run 55 agent scored only 38% against its hard `F` baseline in the final battery and failed its own 52% gate; and the required policy/critic artifacts and per-game rows are not tracked.
- **Henry Charlesworth's settlers_of_catan_RL is the closest published test of a learned/search agent in a full four-player, 10-VP, hidden-information game with player trading**: 47 wins in 100 games for a forward-search wrapper against three copies of its base policy. It has a public checkpoint and a reproducible-looking driver, but no repository license, no retained result file, only one stated seed, and no unaffiliated reproduction. It is source-available, not presently an open-source candidate in the legal sense.
- **BenjaminL1/catan_rl reports the largest well-described within-lineage advantage**—450/600, or 75%, for a pointer policy against its own prior `v11` model, with 300 games per seat and a Wilson interval—but publishes neither checkpoint nor raw log, records no replayable seed list, did not satisfy its second preregistered opening-quality clause, and has no license.
- **Catanatron remains the most useful maintained, immediately executable reference baseline**, not a proven strongest agent. Its only compact strength result is 53–47 over 100 two-player games for depth-2 alpha-beta versus its own value heuristic, without seeds, raw outcomes, or a confidence interval.
- **nogulong/rust-catan-rl is an important replication target**, because it has current Rust code, honest observations, bounded bilateral trading, two ONNX models, and a chair-rotating JSettlers harness. It publishes no completed evaluation output, and the advertised harness is broken at the audited revision.
- **JSettlers2/STAC are mature control implementations, not evidenced winners.** They offer long-lived rules-based, planning, MCTS, and trading infrastructure, but their repositories do not contain a result bundle that identifies an evaluated agent, opponent, sample, seeds, seats, and metric.
- **SamiKoneru/Catan_bot, kvombatkere/Catan-AI, and Algorhythm-sxv/monte-catano do not yet have auditable strength evidence.** The first has an unsupported 94% README claim and no checkpoint/log; the second is an unfinished heuristic/RL prototype; the third is a promising 2026 Rust MCTS MVP with a single commit-message Elo claim, no retained match record, and material rules departures.

One current executable result was independently rerun after the source audit:
Eli6th's shipped 10-VP AlphaBot command produced 83/100 wins, and a seed-777
192-game run produced 157/192 (81.8%). Its historical first-to-7 training and
headline protocol were not reproduced because required source, checkpoint, and
evaluation inputs are absent. No cross-project result was reproduced under one
shared ruleset, so the honest answer remains a shortlist by **evidentiary
role**, not one universal champion.

## Scope and evidence rules

This audit is frozen at **2026-09-05** and covers exactly these repositories: Eli6th/catan-rl, nogulong/rust-catan-rl, bcollazo/Catanatron, henrycharlesworth/settlers_of_catan_RL, jdmonin/JSettlers2, ruflab/StacSettlers, BenjaminL1/catan_rl, kvombatkere/Catan-AI, SamiKoneru/Catan_bot, PeterLP123/catanatron-1v1, and Algorhythm-sxv/monte-catano. STAC is separated from JSettlers2 because the current JSettlers developer documentation points to `ruflab/StacSettlers` as the maintained public STAC code line ([JSettlers developer notes](https://github.com/jdmonin/JSettlers2/blob/12baebade8e206367b4b770de6d5a8b40fc5d726/doc/Readme.developer.md#L1230-L1247)).

Only primary repository evidence was used: source, versioned documentation, commit history, releases, experiment summaries, logs, and model files. README superlatives such as "strongest" or "powerful" are treated as claims, not findings. No result was upgraded from a paper, blog repost, leaderboard, social post, or third-party description.

For this memo:

- **Exact variant** means player count, winning VP, information boundary, trading capability, and any consequential nonstandard rules. An engine's supported rules do not automatically describe a reported experiment.
- **Sample** means completed scored games associated with the result—not training steps, decisions, simulations, or a command's default. If the repository does not connect a default to the quoted result, the sample is recorded as unknown.
- **Chair rotation** means a documented schedule that deliberately evaluates every seat. Random shuffling is recorded as randomization, not balanced rotation.
- **Independent reproduction** requires an unaffiliated party to rerun the exact public artifact/commit under the same rules, opponent versions, chance schedule, chairs, and sample plan, and to publish at least per-game outcomes plus an aggregate. A fork, code reuse, an author's own confirmatory run, or a README statement is not independent reproduction.
- **Open source** requires an explicit software license. A publicly readable repository with no license is source-available but gives no general permission to use, modify, and redistribute it.

Evidence grades describe support for a **strength result**, not code quality:

| Grade | Minimum evidence |
|---|---|
| A | Exact unaffiliated reproduction of a pinned public artifact and protocol. |
| B | Pinned runnable artifact and protocol, with raw per-game outcomes, seeds/chance schedule, seats, and enough environment detail to rerun. |
| C | Author-reported aggregate with at least an identifiable agent, opponent, variant, and sample, but material artifact or protocol gaps. |
| D | Implementation, planned evaluation, or strength claim without an auditable result bundle. |

No candidate reaches A or B. A detailed aggregate can still be C; public weights without result rows can still be C; a sophisticated engine with no retained result remains D.

## Candidate audits

### 1. Eli6th/catan-rl

**Audited revision and maintenance.** Revision [`021279c`](https://github.com/Eli6th/catan-rl/commit/021279c56834b6203480e5292e1de7246e47bd68), dated 2026-06-11. The repository was created and last updated on its default branch that day, so it is recent but has no maintenance history long enough to establish stability. The repository carries an [MIT license](https://github.com/Eli6th/catan-rl/blob/021279c56834b6203480e5292e1de7246e47bd68/LICENSE).

**Exact game variant.** The result ledger says the principal evaluations are four-player, first-to-7, perfect-information games. The trained policy occupies player/seat 0 and faces three fixed H1 heuristic opponents; random seeds vary within a run, while the named fixed-seed promotion gate uses seed 777 ([experiment protocol](https://github.com/Eli6th/catan-rl/blob/021279c56834b6203480e5292e1de7246e47bd68/training/results/EXPERIMENTS.md#L3-L12)). There is no chair rotation. The engine also supports a "realistic" masked observation mode, but that is not the reported Alpha result ([engine observation modes](https://github.com/Eli6th/catan-rl/blob/021279c56834b6203480e5292e1de7246e47bd68/rust/README.md#L23-L29)).

The game supports settlements, cities, roads, robber, development cards, Largest Army, Longest Road, bank/port trading, and bounded player-to-player offers ([engine scope](https://github.com/Eli6th/catan-rl/blob/021279c56834b6203480e5292e1de7246e47bd68/rust/README.md#L3-L7)). P2P offers are limited to giving one or two units of one resource for one unit of another, with at most three generated offers ([trade bounds](https://github.com/Eli6th/catan-rl/blob/021279c56834b6203480e5292e1de7246e47bd68/rust/README.md#L81-L85)). AlphaBot's action filter suppresses trade proposals whenever another legal action exists, so nominal support should not be read as unrestricted negotiation in the evaluated policy.

There is an important replay trap. The Rust engine documents 10 VP as its default and 7 VP as a training target ([target documentation](https://github.com/Eli6th/catan-rl/blob/021279c56834b6203480e5292e1de7246e47bd68/rust/README.md#L66-L68)); the core constant is 10 ([default state target](https://github.com/Eli6th/catan-rl/blob/021279c56834b6203480e5292e1de7246e47bd68/rust/catan-core/src/state.rs#L12-L14)). The README's published Alpha command has no target-VP argument ([command](https://github.com/Eli6th/catan-rl/blob/021279c56834b6203480e5292e1de7246e47bd68/README.md#L60-L70)), and the command-line parser does not expose one. As written, that command evaluates first-to-10, not the headline first-to-7 condition. The reported result therefore cannot be exactly replayed from the quickstart.

**Algorithm and opponent.** AlphaBot combines a learned policy prior with top-k action selection and batched rollout search; the headline configuration is described as prior top-8, 96 full rollouts, and a 300-node/limit field in the CLI configuration. It is compared with three frozen H1 heuristics. This is not a self-play round robin or a test against independently developed strong agents. The README's summary table separately lists random, H1, H2, rollout, PPO, and Alpha agents, with Alpha at 82% ([reported table](https://github.com/Eli6th/catan-rl/blob/021279c56834b6203480e5292e1de7246e47bd68/README.md#L3-L22)).

**Sample, seeds, chairs, and metric.** The metric is seat-0 game win rate. The ledger's general protocol calls the primary gate 192 games at fixed seed 777, but the Alpha design table describes 120–150 games per variant ([Alpha experiments](https://github.com/Eli6th/catan-rl/blob/021279c56834b6203480e5292e1de7246e47bd68/training/results/EXPERIMENTS.md#L68-L88)). The repository does not resolve whether the 82% figure used 120, 150, or 192 completed games. No per-chair results exist because the policy remains in seat 0. The training evaluator likewise fixes the policy in the first seat ([PPO seat construction](https://github.com/Eli6th/catan-rl/blob/021279c56834b6203480e5292e1de7246e47bd68/training/ppo.py#L119-L148)). An older Elo process uses randomized tables/seats and 576 games, but later Alpha checkpoints were not added to that ladder; it is different evidence, not confirmation of 82% ([Elo and Alpha notes](https://github.com/Eli6th/catan-rl/blob/021279c56834b6203480e5292e1de7246e47bd68/training/results/EXPERIMENTS.md#L62-L84)).

**Artifacts and provenance.** The repository tracks a roughly 13 MB PyTorch checkpoint, `models/catan-512-best.pt`, a CTNN export, sample replay files, and `elo-ladder.json`; the README explicitly advertises the model, CTNN, and replays ([artifacts](https://github.com/Eli6th/catan-rl/blob/021279c56834b6203480e5292e1de7246e47bd68/README.md#L87-L98)). The audited PyTorch file has SHA-256 `c4258f235c673efe6968b0aa4de66a1411f01012fa14fe71f548dfff681525a3` and embeds useful metadata: global step 46,497,792, target 7, perfect information, hidden size 512, seed 0, and seat mix `policy, heuristic, policy, heuristic_v2`. It also names engine commit `eb8da245d2420876aae9ea66f60fc62cb1f3e787`, which is not present in the public repository history. That does not prove the model is invalid, but it prevents source-exact reconstruction. The repository says full logs live under `training/results/logs/` and `runs/`, yet those raw logs are not tracked; the checked-in ledger is the only result record ([claimed result files](https://github.com/Eli6th/catan-rl/blob/021279c56834b6203480e5292e1de7246e47bd68/training/results/EXPERIMENTS.md#L9-L12)).

**Evidence verdict.** Grade C. This is the strongest combination of a high
four-player author-reported result and public weights in the set. Its current
first-party 10-VP executable result was rerun unaffiliated at 83/100 and
157/192; its historical first-to-7 result, training lineage, hidden-information
strength, and cross-project strength remain unreproduced.

### 2. nogulong/rust-catan-rl

**Audited revision and maintenance.** Revision [`f734fc7`](https://github.com/nogulong/rust-catan-rl/commit/f734fc74edb707c52f3d59669b54145e274a287d), dated 2026-06-25. The main repository is [MIT-licensed](https://github.com/nogulong/rust-catan-rl/blob/f734fc74edb707c52f3d59669b54145e274a287d/LICENSE); its bundled JSettlers material remains GPL-licensed, so redistributors must inspect the boundary rather than assume the MIT label covers that code.

**Exact game variant.** The intended headline harness is four-player, first-to-10 play: one ONNX model against three JSettlers robots. Training defaults to four players ([training defaults](https://github.com/nogulong/rust-catan-rl/blob/f734fc74edb707c52f3d59669b54145e274a287d/script/train.py#L1387-L1403)), and the game ends at 10 VP ([terminal rule](https://github.com/nogulong/rust-catan-rl/blob/f734fc74edb707c52f3d59669b54145e274a287d/catan/src/game/apply.rs#L422-L430)). The Python observation format defaults to hidden-information-safe observations: exact own holdings, but only public/totals for opponents. Trading includes bank/port trades and bounded bilateral offers of 1-for-1, 2-for-1, and 1-for-2; by default a player may propose one generated P2P offer per turn.

**Algorithm and opponent.** The candidate is PPO actor-critic training with a separate trade-acceptance model, shallow trade lookahead/top-k filtering, and archived policy opponents. The internal four-player training arrangement updates two current-policy seats and draws two seats from the archive. The external evaluation advertised in the README is the model versus three JSettlers SMART bots, with a FAST option ([evaluation command](https://github.com/nogulong/rust-catan-rl/blob/f734fc74edb707c52f3d59669b54145e274a287d/README.md#L35-L43)).

**Sample, seeds, chairs, and metric.** The README promises a 10,000-game JSON evaluation, but no generated JSON or aggregate metric is committed. The external harness cycles the model's chair with `(match_id - 1) % 4`, uses three JSettlers opponents, applies a ten-minute game timeout, and is designed to emit JSONL game/trade records ([external harness](https://github.com/nogulong/rust-catan-rl/blob/f734fc74edb707c52f3d59669b54145e274a287d/script/run_experiment_1vs3.py#L191-L296)). Exact chance seeds and a completed chair distribution are unknown because there is no run output. The internal evaluator is different: 32-game batches, candidate fixed in player 0 against three frozen model seats, with timeouts after 3,000 steps counted as non-wins ([training evaluation](https://github.com/nogulong/rust-catan-rl/blob/f734fc74edb707c52f3d59669b54145e274a287d/script/train.py#L759-L855)). Neither protocol supplies a published strength number.

The advertised external command is not runnable unchanged at the audited revision. The child script writes `model_path_1` although that name is undefined in its result path, and the parallel launcher passes `--model` while the child accepts `--main_model`. These are straightforward repairable defects, but repairing them would create a new experiment rather than reproduce a retained result.

**Artifacts and provenance.** `main_model.onnx` and `trade_model.onnx` are public. Their audited SHA-256 values are `ea2710873255680ae580f1703ded194656e5dad6c39d0b91da6e07b0a5f8cd1c` and `253ad9ac848d9781c5b717b6d0d762f6f6c4cd887741995576726c37cd376fa5`, respectively. No manifest ties them to a training commit, seed set, step count, or the promised 10,000 games; there is no checkpoint series or result output.

**Evidence verdict.** Grade D. This is a credible artifact and harness candidate, but it has no reported completed metric to audit and no independent reproduction.

### 3. Catanatron

**Audited revision and maintenance.** Revision [`d3f4ad0`](https://github.com/bcollazo/catanatron/commit/d3f4ad05bb78d8b2309631d6d3cfa8fcb6fda816), dated 2026-07-15. Catanatron is actively maintained relative to most projects here and is [GPL-3.0 licensed](https://github.com/bcollazo/catanatron/blob/d3f4ad05bb78d8b2309631d6d3cfa8fcb6fda816/LICENSE).

**Exact game variant.** The reported comparison is two-player and uses the engine's ordinary 10-VP terminal target. The base engine supports up to four players, official spiral setup, discard at seven, and no friendly robber by default ([game defaults](https://github.com/bcollazo/catanatron/blob/d3f4ad05bb78d8b2309631d6d3cfa8fcb6fda816/catanatron/catanatron/game.py#L95-L128)). It should not be labeled an honest hidden-information search result: each `Player` receives the complete `Game` object ([player interface](https://github.com/bcollazo/catanatron/blob/d3f4ad05bb78d8b2309631d6d3cfa8fcb6fda816/catanatron/catanatron/models/player.py#L37-L44)). Many heuristic features use own exact holdings and opponent public totals, but the engine does not enforce an observation boundary around the search tree.

Domestic trade is represented as an out-of-band offer flow rather than an ordinary generated action ([game action flow](https://github.com/bcollazo/catanatron/blob/d3f4ad05bb78d8b2309631d6d3cfa8fcb6fda816/catanatron/catanatron/game.py#L20-L48)). The alpha-beta player chooses among generated playable actions and does not originate those offers, so the reported search matchup is effectively bank/port-only from the bot's perspective.

**Algorithm and opponent.** The agent is a depth-2 alpha-beta/expectation search with expected values over chance children and a hand-built state evaluator. The baseline is the same repository's direct value-function player ([search implementation](https://github.com/bcollazo/catanatron/blob/d3f4ad05bb78d8b2309631d6d3cfa8fcb6fda816/catanatron/catanatron/players/minimax.py#L14-L76)).

**Sample, seeds, chairs, and metric.** The project's primary write-up reports 53 wins for depth-2 alpha-beta and 47 for the value player in 100 games ([result](https://github.com/bcollazo/catanatron/blob/d3f4ad05bb78d8b2309631d6d3cfa8fcb6fda816/docs/BLOG_POST.md#L177-L200)). No seed list, game rows, draw/truncation policy, or uncertainty interval is given. Catanatron randomly shuffles player colors when a game is created; this is random seating, not a guaranteed 50/50 chair schedule ([state initialization](https://github.com/bcollazo/catanatron/blob/d3f4ad05bb78d8b2309631d6d3cfa8fcb6fda816/catanatron/catanatron/state.py#L97-L103)).

**Artifacts and provenance.** The complete search source and evaluator constants are the artifact; no learned checkpoint is required. The 100 individual outcomes and their chance seeds are absent.

**Evidence verdict.** Grade C. It is a valuable reproducible code baseline with a thin result record, not evidence of supremacy over the learned/search candidates. No independent exact reproduction was found.

### 4. henrycharlesworth/settlers_of_catan_RL

**Audited revision and maintenance.** Revision [`62a1c04`](https://github.com/henrycharlesworth/settlers_of_catan_RL/commit/62a1c04ec26c2dacc3bbe16cb05975569560b9d2), dated 2022-04-06. No `LICENSE` file or license declaration is present at that revision, so this is not legally open source despite public code and weights.

**Exact game variant.** This is four-player, first-to-10 Catan with hidden information, bank/port trading, and bilateral proposals/responses. Its action heads can propose trades involving bounded combinations of up to four resource units, and observations expose exact own resources while representing opponent cards through public counts and inferred ranges ([author's architecture and rules description](https://settlers-rl.github.io/)). Games truncate after 2,500 decisions in the environment. The forward-search result places one search-enhanced policy against three copies of the unsearched final policy.

**Algorithm and opponent.** The base agent is multi-head PPO trained in semi-asynchronous self-play against past policy versions. The author reports roughly 3,500 training updates and 450 million environment decisions, with 1,200-game periodic evaluations against three copies of older checkpoints ([project report](https://settlers-rl.github.io/)). The final wrapper performs root-only UCB forward search, samples/determinizes uncertain opponent holdings, and uses the final PPO policy for rollouts. The evaluated configuration used 32 processes and 10 seconds of base thinking time per decision ([repository quickstart](https://github.com/henrycharlesworth/settlers_of_catan_RL/blob/62a1c04ec26c2dacc3bbe16cb05975569560b9d2/README.md#L27-L41)).

**Sample, seeds, chairs, and metric.** The reported metric is 47 wins in 100 games for the forward-search player versus three final-policy players ([author's result](https://settlers-rl.github.io/)). The evaluation driver sets Python, NumPy, and Torch seed 10, defaults to 100 games, 32 workers, and 10 seconds, then saves a local output file ([driver configuration](https://github.com/henrycharlesworth/settlers_of_catan_RL/blob/62a1c04ec26c2dacc3bbe16cb05975569560b9d2/evaluation/run_forward_search_evaluation.py#L13-L31)). It randomly shuffles agent-to-color assignments per game rather than constructing an exact every-chair schedule. No per-game output, confidence interval, or repeated-seed run is tracked.

**Artifacts and provenance.** The repository includes `RL/results/default_after_update_3825.pt`, a roughly 7.4 MB checkpoint with audited SHA-256 `1c2f9d40cfc4a1f932356ea342cbfcd86aab4a619a67a4e3775440eeb57004bd`; the README points users to the included checkpoint ([checkpoint](https://github.com/henrycharlesworth/settlers_of_catan_RL/blob/62a1c04ec26c2dacc3bbe16cb05975569560b9d2/README.md#L15-L25)). The repository does not explicitly pin that filename as the exact "final policy" behind the 47/100 report, and the driver's `forward_policy_evaluation.pt` output is absent.

**Evidence verdict.** Grade C for the result, but **ineligible for an open-source winner until licensed**. It is unusually relevant to standard four-player hidden-information play, yet the test is only against its own base policy and has no independent reproduction.

## Evidence-weighted shortlist

This shortlist is intentionally partitioned by what each project can currently substantiate.

1. **Best candidate for reproducing a public four-player learned model: Eli6th/catan-rl.** It has public weights and the highest reported four-player number here, but the immediate reproduction question is only “does this artifact achieve the stated first-to-7, perfect-information seat-0 result against exact H1?”, not “is it best at Catan?”
2. **Best candidate for an evaluation-method template: PeterLP123/catanatron-1v1.** Shared schedules, explicit seats, frozen parents, intervals, machine-readable summaries, and published failed gates are exemplary. It needs the missing weights and raw rows, and it answers only its 2-player/15-VP/balanced-dice track.
3. **Best full-game four-player hidden-information research target: settlers_of_catan_RL, conditional on licensing.** Its 47/100 search result exercises the most relevant rules mix in this audit. The result needs exact artifact linkage, every-chair pairing, more seeds, and an explicit license before it can enter an open-source comparison.
4. **Best maintained executable baseline: Catanatron.** It is suitable as a common host and transparent heuristic/search control. Its 53/100 result is too small and under-recorded to crown the alpha-beta bot.
5. **Best near-term standard-rules learned-agent replication target: nogulong/rust-catan-rl.** It already has honest observations, P2P mechanics, and ONNX models. The missing work is to repair and freeze the harness, identify model provenance, and publish the first actual outcome set.
6. **Useful controls rather than title candidates: JSettlers2 and STAC.** Their value is mature bot/trading infrastructure and historically relevant opponents. A benchmark must be created before a strength claim exists.

BenjaminL1/catan_rl would join the 1v1 shortlist if its author publishes a license, the two named checkpoints, the historical engine revision, and raw seeded outcomes. SamiKoneru/Catan_bot would need the same basics plus proof of its exact sample and trading path. monte-catano needs rules conformance and a retained SPRT record before speed or Elo claims can influence selection. kvombatkere/Catan-AI is not sufficiently complete for a strength reproduction.

## What must be reproduced before calling any agent “strongest”

The word **strongest** must first be scoped to a frozen track. At minimum, use separate titles for:

1. **Standard four-player:** base board, first to 10 VP, ordinary 2d6, honest hidden information, finite bank, standard discard/Largest Army/robber rules, and full bank/port plus P2P trading.
2. **Four-player restricted-information/action tracks:** perfect information and/or no-P2P play, each labeled separately rather than treated as standard Catan.
3. **Two-player 15-VP track:** the exact Colonist-style rules, dice scheduler, discard threshold, friendly-robber threshold, and P2P policy must be frozen. Ordinary-dice and balanced-dice results remain separate tracks.

Within one track, all of the following are required:

- Freeze the engine commit, rules/config file, agent source, weights/model hashes, dependency lockfiles, hardware class, per-move time or simulation budget, and deterministic build/container.
- Verify rules conformance with tests for setup, terminal VP, bank shortages, robber/discard, development-card timing, awards, ports, hidden observations, and trade legality. Instrument the observation boundary to prove that hidden opponent state cannot affect a decision except through legal public inference.
- Port every finalist into one audited engine where practical. If cross-engine ports are impossible, publish deterministic conformance traces and use matched chance tapes only where the engines assign identical semantics to every random event.
- Use a held-out master schedule. For four-player head-to-head/table tests, use at least **1,000 master chance seeds × every candidate chair = 4,000 scored games per matchup/table configuration**. For two-player tests, use at least **1,000 master seeds × both seat assignments = 2,000 games per matchup**. Predeclare timeout/truncation handling; count an agent-caused timeout or illegal move as a loss.
- Test finalists against every other finalist, frozen simple controls, and relevant external controls (at least Catanatron and JSettlers where rules permit).
